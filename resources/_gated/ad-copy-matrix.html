The Ad Copy Matrix: 40 frameworks for D2C ads on Meta

Script structures that will help you turn attention into sales. Every framework is broken down, tagged by format and level of awareness, with descriptions of why they work and examples you can brief from.

On this page

Why copy matters now

Copy used to be how you persuaded someone to buy. Now it's also how you tell Meta who the ad is for. With broad targeting, the algorithm reads your creative to decide who to show it to, so clear, specific messaging means better delivery and more stable performance.

That's why the framework comes third, not first. Every strong ad is built in the same order:

  1. 01Insight

    What your customer already thinks or feels.

  2. 02Message

    The single idea this ad will land.

  3. 03Structure

    The framework that carries it. This page.

  4. 04Execution

    The words, the tone and the format.

⚡

The one rule: one framework per ad, matched to the problem, not the product. Pick the framework after the insight, never before. Forcing a framework onto a weak insight just gives you a well-structured ad that says nothing.

⚠️

The worked examples are illustrations. Swap in your own numbers, reviews and claims, and only say what you can prove. In health & wellness especially, every claim needs to stand up to Meta's ad review and the ASA.

The five levels of awareness

Every framework is tagged with the levels it works best for.

  1. Unaware

    They think: “This is just how things are.”

    Your copy's job: make them realise it's a problem worth solving.

  2. Problem aware

    They think: “I need to fix this, but nothing works.”

    Your copy's job: show you understand, then introduce a better way.

  3. Solution aware

    They think: “I know what's out there. Why this one?”

    Your copy's job: differentiate, and win the comparison.

  4. Product aware

    They think: “Does it actually work for people like me?”

    Your copy's job: prove it with proof that isn't yours.

  5. Most aware

    They think: “I want it. I just haven't bought yet.”

    Your copy's job: remove the last objection and give a reason to act now.

Section 1: Copy formulas

7 formulas that structure any ad, in any format

These are the building blocks. Each one works as a video script, as the copy on a static, or as primary text. Pick the one that matches your persona's awareness level and the problem you're solving, then fill in the beats.

Best for

Cold, broad audiences and simple products where clarity matters most.

Avoid when

Products that need a lot of explaining or proof. Use FAB or PPPP instead.

BeatWhat to writeWhy it works
AttentionOne line that names the moment or the outcome.Nothing downstream matters if the first line doesn't stop the scroll.
InterestThe surprising detail: what it is, how it works, why it's different.Curiosity buys you the next five seconds.
DesirePaint the outcome. This should be the longest beat.People buy the after, not the ingredients. Cut Interest before you cut Desire.
ActionOne clear next step, with a reason to take it now.A vague CTA leaks the desire you just built.

Fill-in template

[Attention: the moment or outcome]. [Interest: what makes this different]. [Desire: what life looks like after]. [Action: the one thing to do next].

Health & wellness

Third coffee and still flagging by 3pm? This is a 30-second morning drink with lion's mane and B vitamins, and no caffeine crash. Imagine getting to 6pm with energy left for the gym. Try your first box today.

Alcohol-free

Want the Friday-night feeling without the Saturday? This is a botanical 0% spritz, bittersweet and properly grown-up. Imagine waking up on Saturday with the whole day still ahead of you. Grab a mixed case and find your favourite.

Pairs well with Hook Matrix #1 The daily moment, #21 The real cause

Best for

Problem-aware audiences with a real pain they've come to accept as normal.

Avoid when

Aspirational products with no real pain. Use AIDA or BAB instead.

BeatWhat to writeWhy it works
ProblemName the specific problem, in their words.Recognition is the fastest form of trust.
AgitateShow what it's really costing them, and what they've tried that failed.The make-or-break beat. Without agitation, the solution feels optional.
SolutionIntroduce the product as the obvious answer, with one reason it works.Relief lands harder after tension.

Fill-in template

[The problem, in their words]. [What it's really costing them, and what they've already tried]. [The product, and why it works when the rest didn't].

Health & wellness

Bloated by 4pm every single day? You've cut bread, tried the teas, bought the gummies, and you still undo your top button after lunch. Our daily gut blend works on the cause, not the symptom, with five strains studied specifically for bloating.

Alcohol-free

Hungover after two glasses? It's not you getting older. And “just the one” stopped being just the one a while ago. Our 0% reds are made like real wine, then gently de-alcoholised, so you keep the taste and lose the Sunday.

Pairs well with Hook Matrix #11 The graveyard, #16 The wrong diagnosis

Best for

When the pull of the “after” is stronger than the push of the pain. Lifestyle products, UGC and testimonials.

Avoid when

Vague befores. “I was tired” doesn't work. “I was asleep on the sofa by 8pm” does.

BeatWhat to writeWhy it works
BeforeA specific, recognisable scene from their life now.Specific befores qualify the right viewer.
AfterThe same scene, transformed.Desire comes from seeing the gap.
BridgeThe product is how you get from one to the other.The product earns its place as the bridge, not the hero.

Fill-in template

Before: [a specific scene from their life now]. After: [the same scene, transformed]. Bridge: [the product, and how it gets you there].

Health & wellness

Six months ago I was asleep on the sofa by 8pm. Now I'm out for a run after work. The only thing I changed? Swapping my afternoon coffee for this.

Alcohol-free

Last summer every barbecue ended with me on the sofa by 9. This summer I'm the last one at the table. Same parties, same friends, and a fridge full of 0% IPAs that actually taste like beer.

Pairs well with Hook Matrix #5 The countdown, #34 The diary

Best for

Products with a real technical difference, and audiences comparing options.

Avoid when

Cold audiences. And never stop at the advantage: always land the benefit.

BeatWhat to writeWhy it works
FeatureWhat it is or has. One specific detail.Specifics read as credible.
AdvantageWhat that feature does that the alternatives don't.This is your differentiation.
BenefitWhat that means for their life.People buy outcomes. A feature without a benefit is a spec sheet.

Fill-in template

[Feature]. That means [the advantage over alternatives]. So you [the benefit in their life].

Health & wellness

Our magnesium is glycinate, not oxide. That means your body actually absorbs it, rather than flushing most of it out. So you fall asleep faster and wake up without the fog.

Alcohol-free

Our gin is distilled with 11 botanicals, then the alcohol is removed at a low temperature. That keeps the flavour most 0% spirits lose. So your G&T tastes like a G&T, not tonic with a rumour of juniper.

Pairs well with Hook Matrix #22 The label check, #36 The blind test

Best for

Claims that sound too good to be true, where proof does the heavy lifting.

Avoid when

Any time you don't have real, verifiable proof.

BeatWhat to writeWhy it works
PicturePaint the desired outcome vividly.Visualisation creates want before the claim arrives.
PromiseMake the specific claim.A clear promise gives the proof something to land on.
ProofNumbers, reviews, studies, before and afters.Proof turns a claim into a fact.
PushA direct ask, with a reason to act now.Momentum fades fast. Ask while belief is highest.

Fill-in template

Picture [the outcome]. [Product] [the specific promise]. [Proof: a number, review or study]. [Push: act now because…].

Health & wellness

Picture waking up before your alarm, actually rested. Our sleep blend helps you fall asleep faster and stay asleep. 4,000 five-star reviews, and most customers reorder within a month. Try it risk-free for 30 days.

Alcohol-free

Picture a Friday night out where you remember every conversation. Our 0% aperitif gives you the ritual without the fog. Over 11,000 bottles sold in Dry January alone. Your first bottle ships free this week.

Pairs well with Hook Matrix #32 The exact result, #38 The crowd

Best for

Premium products, subscriptions and anything over about £50. Founder-led and UGC video.

Avoid when

Short placements like Stories. It needs 25 to 45 seconds to breathe.

BeatWhat to writeWhy it works
ProblemName the problem.Qualifies the viewer.
AmplifyShow the cost of leaving it unsolved.Raises the stakes so the price feels small.
SolutionIntroduce the product and how it works.The mechanism builds belief.
TransformationReal results and testimonials.Proof that it happens to people like them.
OfferWhat they get, the price, the guarantee.Clarity removes friction at the decision point.
ResponseTell them exactly what to do next.High-consideration buyers need a clear, low-risk next step.

Fill-in template

[Problem]. [What it costs if nothing changes]. [Product, and how it works]. [A real result from a real customer]. [Offer: what's included, price, guarantee]. [Exactly what to do next].

Health & wellness

Hormonal skin at 35? It's not just breakouts. It's cancelling plans and caking on concealer every morning. Our 3-step routine targets the hormonal cause with zinc and spearmint. Jess saw clearer skin in 8 weeks. Your first month is £29, with a 60-day guarantee. Tap below to start.

Alcohol-free

Still drinking because it's easier than explaining why you're not? That's every wedding, every work do, every Friday. Our 0% range is made by brewers, not chemists. Tom's been alcohol-free for a year and still never misses the pub. A mixed case of 24 is £36 with free delivery. Pick yours below.

Pairs well with Hook Matrix #6 The calendar dread, #44 Them vs you

Best for

Retargeting, objection-led ads, comment replies and price pushback.

Avoid when

Cold audiences who don't have the objection yet. You'll plant it.

BeatWhat to writeWhy it works
FeelAcknowledge the doubt: “I get why you'd think that.”Agreement lowers defences.
FeltShow others felt the same, ideally a real customer.It normalises the doubt, so it stops feeling like a red flag.
FoundWhat they found once they tried it.Peer proof beats brand claims.

Fill-in template

[It's normal to think: the objection]. [Real customers thought the same]. [What they found once they tried it].

Health & wellness

Think greens powders all taste like pond water? Fair, most do. Sarah thought the same. Her review: “Tastes like apple juice. I actually look forward to it.”

Alcohol-free

Not sure £1.50 a can is worth it for a beer with no alcohol? Most of our customers felt the same. Then they worked out it's a quarter of the price of a pint at the pub, with none of the hangover.

Section 2: Video scripts

21 script structures, grouped by awareness level

Each structure comes with timed beats, what's on screen, what to say and why each beat works. Timings are a guide for 15 to 45 second ads. Brief the beats, then let creators make the words their own.

Unaware 5 structures

Best for

Cold, broad audiences on Reels and Stories.

Avoid when

Interrupts with nothing to do with the product. Shock without relevance just burns spend.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works
0-2sHookSomething unexpected: the product dropped, sliced, poured awayNothing, or one wordBreaks the scroll pattern before the brain decides to skip.
2-4sBridgeConnect the strange visual to the problem“Here's why I'm doing this…”Gives the viewer a reason for what they just saw.
4-9sValueOne quantified claim on screenThe one stat that mattersA number gives the surprise a payoff.
9-12sCTAProduct and offer“Try it”Short and direct while attention is at its peak.

Worked example · Health & wellness

  1. 0-2s

    Pours a full energy drink down the sink.

  2. 2-4s

    “That's 40g of sugar I'm no longer drinking every afternoon.”

  3. 4-9s

    On screen: “Zero sugar. Same energy. No 4pm crash.”

  4. 9-12s

    “Swap your 3pm can. Link below.”

💡

Pro tip: Film three different interrupts on the same body and test them. The interrupt is the variable.

Best for

Brands with a genuine origin frustration. Nobody else can copy it.

Avoid when

Stories about the founder instead of the customer's problem.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works
0-3sHookFounder to camera, or in-the-moment B-rollA vulnerable or surprising admissionVulnerability earns attention without shouting.
3-12sStruggleB-roll of the old problemThe specific frustration that started it allThe viewer sees their own problem in the founder's.
12-25sThe turnMaking the product, early versionsWhat they built, and why it's differentThe mechanism, told as a story rather than a spec.
25-35sCTAFounder with the productAn invitation, not a hard sellA founder's ask feels personal, not salesy.

Worked example · Alcohol-free

  1. 0-3s

    “I nearly shut this company down in year one.”

  2. 3-12s

    “I'd quit drinking, and every 0% beer I tried tasted like sweet bread water.”

  3. 12-25s

    “So I spent 14 months with a brewer working out how to keep the hops when you take the alcohol out.”

  4. 25-35s

    “If you've given up on alcohol-free beer, give it one more go. Your first case is half price.”

💡

Pro tip: Shoot it on a phone. Over-produced founder stories feel like ads. Raw ones feel like confessions.

Best for

Sound-off feeds and products with a visible, satisfying action.

Avoid when

Products whose benefit can't be seen. If it needs explaining, use a voiceover.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works
0-3sHookASMR close-up: a pour, a fizz, a stirText: the outcome in five wordsSensory fascination holds attention without sound.
3-8sDemoThe satisfying action, extendedText: how it works, in one lineWatching it happen is the proof.
8-11sBenefitsProduct hero shotText: two or three short benefitsShort text reads in a glance.
11-14sCTAPack shotText: the offer and “Shop now”A visual CTA works with the sound off.

Worked example · Health & wellness

  1. 0-3s

    Powder hits water and swirls bright green. Text: “Your greens, done in 10 seconds.”

  2. 3-8s

    Shake, pour over ice. Text: “22 superfoods. Tastes like apple.”

  3. 8-11s

    Text: “No blender. No bitterness. No excuses.”

  4. 11-14s

    Pack shot. Text: “First month 40% off.”

💡

Pro tip: Each text card should read in under two seconds. If you have to pause the video to read it, it's too long.

Best for

Secondary-benefit angles: when the real mechanism is boring but the side effect is socially interesting.

Avoid when

Scripted-sounding delivery. If it sounds like an ad, it's lost the point.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works
0-3sHookCreator mid-moment, POV captionStart in the middle of the storyNative content earns the view. Ads get skipped.

  1. 0-3s

    Text: “POV: you told your mates you're doing a sober month and they planned a pub crawl.”

  2. 3-15s

  3. 15-22s

    “Found this IPA at round three and genuinely couldn't tell. Neither could Dan.”

  4. 22-28s

    “Day 18, no hangovers, still getting invited out. I'll leave the link.”

Best for

Challenger brands taking on a bloated or dishonest category.

Avoid when

Naming competitors. Attack the category or the practice, never a brand.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works
0-3sCall outDirect to cameraName the enemy: an industry practicePicking a side makes people lean in.
3-7sExposeProof of the problem: labels, receiptsWhy the enemy is bad for themOutrage is memorable.
7-12sHeroThe product as the alternativeWhat you do differentlyThe product becomes a stance, not just a purchase.
12-15sJoin usCreator with the product“Join the people who…”Turns a purchase into an identity.

Worked example · Health & wellness

  1. 0-3s

    “The supplement industry is banking on you never reading the back of the tub.”

  2. 3-7s

    “‘Proprietary blend’ means they don't have to tell you how much of anything is in there.”

  3. 7-12s

    “Every dose on our label is printed, tested and matched to the studies.”

  4. 12-15s

    “Join 40,000 people who read the label.”

💡

Pro tip: The enemy has to be real and defensible. If your claim wouldn't survive a journalist checking it, pick a different enemy.

Problem aware 5 structures

Best for

Problem-aware personas with a specific failed solution.

Avoid when

Most-aware audiences, or when there's no specific failed solution to call out.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works
0-3sHookCreator, close upName the specific persona and momentQualifies the right viewer out loud.
3-15sHoldCreator, with B-roll of the struggleTwist the knife, then name what they already triedNaming the failed solution proves you understand them.
15-35sShiftProduct and a mechanism visualWhy the failed thing failed, and how this worksThe mechanism turns hope into belief.
35-45sPayoffThe result, with the productThe specific outcome, then a low-friction CTAEnding on the outcome is what they remember.

Worked example · Health & wellness

  1. 0-3s

    “If you're a new mum surviving on four hours' sleep, stay with me.”

  2. 3-15s

    “I tried melatonin, the apps, lavender everything. I'd still be wide awake at 2am, even when the baby wasn't.”

  3. 15-35s

    “Melatonin tells your body it's night. It doesn't calm a wired nervous system. This has magnesium glycinate and L-theanine, which do.”

  4. 35-45s

    “Now when I get the chance to sleep, I actually do. Link's below.”

💡

Pro tip: “I tried the supermarket one” beats “I tried everything”. The failed solution has to be specific.

Best for

Any product solving a clear, everyday pain.

Avoid when

Products with no real pain. It falls flat without genuine frustration.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works

2-4sTurnProduct reveal“…until I found this”The pivot creates relief.
4-17sBackingB-roll, results, product in useTwo or three USPs and the outcomeProof stacked fast keeps the momentum.
17-20sCTACreator with the product, relieved“If you've got the same problem, try it”Mirrors the hook and closes the loop.

Worked example · Alcohol-free

  1. 0-2s

    “I was so sick of being the only one at the table with a lemonade.”

  2. 2-4s

  3. 4-17s

  4. 17-20s

    “If that's you at every dinner, get the variety pack.”

Best for

Categories full of misinformation: supplements, skincare, nutrition.

Avoid when

Myths you can't disprove with evidence. If it's opinion, it's a hot take, not a myth.

Worked example · Health & wellness

  1. 0-3s

    Text: “MYTH: all collagen is the same.”

  2. 3-12s

    “Most collagen is broken down so much your body treats it like any other protein. Peptide size matters.”

  3. 12-20s

  4. 20-25s

    “Check your label. Then check ours.”

💡

Pro tip: Running several myths? Save your differentiator for the last one, so the product is the punchline.

Best for

Usage-based products and habit changes: routines, recipes, rituals.

Avoid when

Making the viewer feel stupid. The tone is “we all did this”, not “you idiot”.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works
0-3sWrongRed cross over the common habit“Stop doing this”We're wired to correct errors.
3-8sRightGreen tick, the better method with the productThe right way, in stepsShows instead of tells.
8-12sWhyClose-up of the resultWhy it works betterGives the habit change a reason.

Worked example · Health & wellness

  1. 0-3s

    Red cross over a coffee on an empty stomach. “Stop doing this at 7am.”

  2. 3-8s

    Green tick: protein shake first, coffee an hour later.

  3. 8-12s

    “Protein first steadies your blood sugar, so the coffee doesn't send you crashing.”

  4. 12-15s

    “Ours has 25g of protein and tastes like a latte. Start tomorrow.”

💡

Pro tip: Film both halves in the same setting, so the only thing that changes is the method.

Best for

Educating a problem-aware audience out of a bad habit and into your category.

Avoid when

Padding. If you only have two strong reasons, make it two.

TimeBeatOn screenSayWhy it works
0-3sHookCreator, with the number on screen“3 reasons I switched to…”A numbered list promises a finish line, so people stay for it.
3-10sReason 1The most surprising reasonOne reason, one proofSurprise earns the next reason.
10-17sReason 2The most relatable reasonOne reason, one proofRelatability keeps them nodding.
17-25sReason 3 + CTAThe most compelling reason, then the productThe strongest reason, then the askThe last reason is the one they act on.

Worked example · Alcohol-free

  1. 0-3s

    “3 reasons I've swapped wine for this on weeknights.”

  2. 3-10s

    “One: it's 20 calories a glass. My old glass of red was 160.”

  3. 10-17s

  4. 17-25s

    “Three: it tastes like the real thing, so I don't feel like I'm missing out. Link below.”

💡

Solution aware 4 structures

Primary text

Section 4: Primary text

6 patterns for the caption above the ad

Primary text is the copy above your creative. Only the first 125 characters or so show before “See more”, so the opening line matters most.

🔗

The matching rule: the caption should continue the creative, not repeat it. Same angle, same persona, next beat. If the video names the problem, the caption can carry the proof.

The one-liner

Cold, visual-led ads where the creative does the talking.

One short line that continues the hook. No more than 125 characters, so nothing gets cut off.

Example

Your 3pm slump called. It's not coming back.

Benefit bullets

Product-aware viewers scanning for reasons.

A hook line, then three or four short bullets, then the CTA.

Example

The 0% IPA that tastes like an IPA:
• Brewed like real beer
• 58 calories a can
• No Sunday hangover
Grab a mixed case below.

Story-led long form

Founder-led and UGC ads, and cold audiences who like a story.

A first-person story of 80 to 150 words: the moment, what they tried, what changed, the CTA.

Example

Last year I was asleep on the sofa by 8pm most nights. I blamed work, then age, then the kids. I tried more coffee, then less coffee, then a fortune in supplements. What actually changed things was the simplest swap... (keep going: the swap, the result, the link).

Objection-led

Retargeting, when you know what's stopping them.

Open with the doubt in their words, answer it, prove it, ask.

Example

“Won't it just taste like juice?” Fair question. It's made from real wine with the alcohol gently removed, which is why 3,000 reviewers give it 4.7 stars. Try the tasting trio.

Proof stack

Product-aware audiences who need reassurance.

Lead with the strongest review or number, then stack two more pieces of proof.

Example

“I genuinely forgot what 3pm tiredness felt like.” Jess, 5 stars. 40,000 customers. 9 in 10 reorder. See what the fuss is about.

Offer close

Most-aware audiences who are ready to buy.

The offer, the guarantee and a real deadline. Short and specific.

Example

Bundle + free shaker for £39 until Sunday. 60-day money-back guarantee, even if the tub's empty.
