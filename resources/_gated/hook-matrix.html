The Hook Matrix: 60 hook formulas for scroll-stopping Meta ads

Every formula comes with a fill-in template and two worked examples, sorted by the emotion it uses and the level of awareness it's built for. Plus 36 quick-fire openers, a testing protocol and a checklist to run before you brief.

How to use this matrix

Three axes. One rule.

01

Emotion

Pain, frustration, curiosity, proof, identity or urgency. Pick the one your persona is feeling right now, at this point in their journey.

02

Awareness level

Unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware or most aware. This decides how much the viewer already knows, and so what your hook has to do.

03

Persona moment

A great hook names a specific moment, not a general state. Moments qualify the right viewer. States qualify nobody.

The five levels of awareness

From Eugene Schwartz's Breakthrough Advertising. Every section below is built for one of them.

  1. Unaware

    They think: “This is just how things are.”

    Your hook's job: name a moment they recognise, so they realise it's a problem.

  2. Problem aware

    They think: “I need to fix this, but nothing works.”

    Your hook's job: show you understand the problem, and what they've already tried.

  3. Solution aware

    They think: “I know what's out there. Why would this be different?”

    Your hook's job: reframe the category so your approach stands apart.

  4. Product aware

    They think: “I've seen you. Does it actually work for people like me?”

    Your hook's job: prove it, and help them see themselves using it.

  5. Most aware

    They think: “I want it. I just haven't bought yet.”

    Your hook's job: remove the last objection and give them a reason to act now.

💡

Awareness isn't the same as targeting. A broad audience contains people at every level, so let the hook do the qualifying. Retargeting audiences sit mostly at product aware and most aware.

⚡

The one rule: one hook type per ad. Vary the hook type, not just the wording. Three ads with the same argument and different footage are one ad, and you'll never learn what actually worked.

⏱️

Use it in under two minutes: pick your persona's awareness level, find the emotion your persona is feeling, copy the template, fill the brackets with words lifted from real reviews, then brief it.

Unaware Pain

Section 1: Moment hooks

10 formulas for unaware audiences

Unaware audiences don't see their problem as a problem yet, and they've never heard of you. They're scrolling past. A moment hook stops them by describing a scene they lived through this week, so precisely that it feels like you were there.

The difference between a moment and a state is everything. "Tired all the time" is a state, and it qualifies nobody. "On your third coffee before 3pm" is a moment, and the right person recognises themselves instantly.

HookFill-in templateHealth & wellnessAlcohol-free
01The daily moment

If you [specific daily moment], this is for you

 If you're on your third coffee before 3pm and still yawning, this is for you If you've ever nursed one pint all night so you could drive home, this is for you
02The private ritual

Every [time], the same thing: [hidden painful ritual]

 Every night, the same thing: lying awake at 2am working out how many hours are left Every Sunday, the same thing: promising yourself next weekend will be different
03The question that stung

Someone asked me “[awkward question]” and I didn't have an answer

 My mate asked why I'm always knackered and I didn't have an answer Someone asked “why aren't you drinking?” for the fifth time that night
04The realisation

That moment when [hyper-specific sight or realisation]

 That moment you catch your reflection after a full night's sleep and still look exhausted That moment you open your banking app on Sunday and see what Saturday cost
05The countdown

It's [time] and you're already [stuck state]

 It's 10am and you're already counting down to bedtime It's Saturday morning and you've already written off the whole day
06The calendar dread

If [upcoming event] is already making you [feeling], watch this

 If Monday's alarm is already making you anxious on Sunday night, watch this If another wedding invite is already making you dread the open bar, watch this
07The cover-up line

How many times have you said “[cover-up line]” this week?

 How many times have you said “I'm fine, just tired” this week? How many times have you said “just the one” this month?
08The body signal

Your body is telling you something every time you [symptom moment]

 Your gut is telling you something every time you undo your top button after lunch Your body is telling you something every time a two-glass night costs you the next morning
09The false trade-off

You shouldn't have to choose between [want A] and [want B]

 You shouldn't have to choose between hitting your goals and actually enjoying dinner You shouldn't have to choose between a night out and a good Sunday
10The precise call-out

[Very specific person] who [specific situation]: stop scrolling

 New mums running on four hours' sleep: stop scrolling Runners training for a spring marathon who still want the pub: stop scrolling
💡

Pull moments straight from reviews. Search your reviews for times of day, places and people: "at 3pm", "on the school run", "my partner noticed". That's where the moments live, already written in your customer's own words.

Problem aware Frustration

Section 2: Failed-solution hooks

10 formulas for problem-aware audiences

Problem-aware audiences know something's wrong and they're actively trying to fix it. They've almost always tried something already, and it let them down. Naming that specific failed fix is the fastest way to earn trust: you prove you understand their journey before you ask them to try your solution.

Specific beats general every time. "I tried the supermarket one" beats "I tried everything", because only one of them sounds like a real person.

HookFill-in templateHealth & wellnessAlcohol-free
11The graveyard

I've got a cupboard full of [failed products]. Here's what finally worked.

 I've got a cupboard full of half-used gut supplements. Here's what finally worked. I've got a fridge full of alcohol-free beers I couldn't finish. This is the one I reorder.
12The receipt

I spent £[X] on [failed alternative] before I worked out [insight]

 I spent £400 on sleep gadgets before I worked out the problem was my evening routine I spent a fortune on mocktails that tasted like squash before I found this
13The named failure

[Popular fix] didn't work for me. Here's why.

 Melatonin didn't work for me. Here's why it doesn't work for most people. “Just drink sparkling water” didn't work for me. Here's why.
14I did everything right

[Fix one], [fix two], [fix three]. Still [pain].

 Eight hours in bed, no screens, magnesium. Still waking up exhausted. Dry January, sober apps, pure willpower. Still felt left out every Friday.
15The swap

I swapped [old habit] for [new thing] for [time]. Here's what changed.

 I swapped my second coffee for this for 30 days. Here's what changed. I swapped my Friday bottle of wine for this for a month. Here's what changed.
16The wrong diagnosis

If [common fix] hasn't fixed your [pain], it's because [real cause]

 If cutting carbs hasn't fixed your bloating, it's because carbs were never the cause If cutting back hasn't stuck, it's because you miss the ritual, not the alcohol
17The forced compromise

Most [category] make you choose between [A] and [B]. This doesn't.

 Most protein drinks make you choose between taste and clean ingredients. This doesn't. Most alcohol-free wines make you choose between taste and low sugar. This doesn't.
18The low expectation

I expected [disappointing thing]. I was wrong.

 I expected another chalky greens powder. I was wrong. I expected alcohol-free gin to taste like perfumed water. I was wrong.
19The last chance

You've tried [X], [Y] and [Z]. Give me 30 seconds before you give up.

 You've tried the apps, the teas and the gummies. Give me 30 seconds before you give up on sleep. You've tried the apps, the challenges and the cut-down weeks. Give me 30 seconds.
20The workaround confession

I used to [awkward workaround] just to avoid [pain]

 I used to skip 10am meetings just to avoid the mid-morning crash I used to pour lemonade into a wine glass so nobody would ask questions
⚠️

Name the category, never the competitor. "The supermarket one", "chalky powders" and "the big-brand 0%" all land without inviting a complaint. Naming a rival brand in a hook rarely ends well.

Solution aware Curiosity

Section 3: Curiosity & reframe hooks

10 formulas for solution-aware audiences

Solution-aware audiences know products like yours exist. They've seen the category, and they've probably bought from it. To stand out from every option they already know, you need to reframe: tell your persona that what they've been blaming, or buying, isn't the real answer.

Curiosity hooks do that by opening a loop the viewer's brain needs to close. Withhold the answer just long enough to earn the next five seconds, then pay it off. That's the whole mechanism.

HookFill-in templateHealth & wellnessAlcohol-free
21The real cause

It's not [assumed cause]. It's [real cause].

 It's not your age. It's your blood sugar. It's not the alcohol you miss. It's the ritual.
22The label check

Check the back of your [product]. If you see [ingredient], keep watching.

 Check the back of your protein bar. If you see maltitol, keep watching. Check the back of your alcohol-free beer. If sugar is in the top three, keep watching.
23The myth flip

Everyone says [common belief]. The evidence says something different.

 Everyone says breakfast is the most important meal. The evidence is a lot messier. Everyone says a glass of red is good for your heart. The evidence has moved on.
24What happens when

Here's what happens to your [body or life] [time] after you [action]

 Here's what happens to your gut 72 hours after you cut ultra-processed snacks Here's what happens to your sleep seven days after your last drink
25The insider

I've worked in [industry] for [X] years. Here's what [insider truth].

 I've formulated supplements for 10 years. Here's what never makes it onto the label. I've worked in drinks for 12 years. Here's why most alcohol-free beer tastes flat.
26The odd pairing

What do [unexpected thing] and [your product] have in common?

 What do Okinawa's centenarians and your morning drink have in common? What do award-winning sommeliers and a 0% aperitif have in common?
27The not-what-you-think

The one thing I changed that fixed my [pain]. It's not [obvious answer].

 The one thing I changed that fixed my afternoon slump. It's not caffeine. The one thing that made going alcohol-free easy. It's not willpower.
28The self-test

Quick test: [simple self-check]. If [result], keep watching.

 Quick test: do you need caffeine to feel normal before 10am? If yes, keep watching. Quick test: do you check the drinks menu before you book a restaurant? If yes, keep watching.
29The quiet saboteur

The [habit] that's quietly undoing your [goal]

 The bedtime habit that's quietly undoing your workouts The “one drink” habit that's quietly costing you your weekends
30The unasked question

Why does nobody talk about [overlooked truth]?

 Why does nobody talk about how much your gut affects your mood? Why does nobody talk about how lonely your first sober night out feels?
💡

A curiosity hook has to pay off. If the answer never arrives in the body of the ad, you'll get a great hook rate, a terrible hold rate and a viewer who feels tricked. Open the loop, then close it.

Product aware Proof

Section 4: Proof hooks

10 formulas for product-aware audiences

Product-aware audiences know who you are and what you sell. Now they're weighing up whether you're the answer. Vague proof like "loved by thousands" doesn't move them. Specific proof does: exact numbers, real names and real words lifted straight from reviews.

Precise numbers read as real. Round numbers read as marketing.

HookFill-in templateHealth & wellnessAlcohol-free
31The review read-out

Open on a real review, read word for word: “[exact customer quote]”

 “I genuinely forgot what 3pm tiredness felt like.” Jess, five-star review “My husband didn't notice it wasn't real beer until I told him.” Sarah, verified buyer
32The exact result

[Specific number] [result] in [specific time]

 Falling asleep 22 minutes faster within the first fortnight Six alcohol-free weekends and 3 kilos lighter
33The reorder signal

[X]% of customers reorder within [time]

 62% of our customers reorder before their first tub runs out Most people who try the mixed case come back for a full case of one flavour
34The diary

Day 1 vs day [X] of [using the product]

 Day 1 vs day 30 of swapping my afternoon coffee Week 1 vs week 8 of alcohol-free Fridays
35The converted sceptic

I was the biggest sceptic of [category]. Then [turning point].

 I rolled my eyes at greens powders for years. Then my blood test came back. I thought alcohol-free beer was pointless. Then I tried it blind at a barbecue.
36The blind test

We gave [people] [our product] and [alternative] without labels. [Result].

 We gave 20 coffee drinkers our blend and their usual without labels. 14 picked ours. We poured our 0% next to a craft IPA at a pub quiz. Half the room got it wrong.
37The expert nod

Why [credible professionals] keep recommending [ingredient or product]

 Why dietitians keep recommending this one ingredient for bloating Why bartenders at award-winning bars are stocking this 0% spirit
38The crowd

[Number] people switched to [product] in [time]

 4,200 people swapped their morning coffee for this last month 11,000 bottles sold during Dry January alone
39The first reaction

Their face when they [experience the product] for the first time

 My dad's face when his first protein shake didn't taste like chalk My friends' faces when I told them the spritz they were drinking was 0%
40The review stack

Three short real reviews, back to back, no intro

 “Actually works.” / “On my fourth tub.” / “My partner keeps stealing it.” “Tastes like the real thing.” / “Ordered a second case.” / “Saved my Sundays.”
⚠️

Only use numbers and quotes you can prove. The figures above are illustrations, so swap in your own data. In health & wellness especially, every claim needs to stand up to Meta's ad review and the ASA.

Product aware Identity

Section 5: Identity & call-out hooks

10 formulas for product-aware audiences

Product-aware audiences know what you sell but haven't decided it's for them. People buy things that confirm who they are, or who they're becoming. Identity hooks hold up a mirror: if the viewer recognises themselves, they lean in.

Call out a specific type of person, a value they hold, or the version of themselves they're working towards.

HookFill-in templateHealth & wellnessAlcohol-free
41The tribe call-out

If you're the kind of person who [specific behaviour], you'll get this

 If you're the kind of person who reads every ingredient list in the supermarket, you'll get this If you're the kind of person who still wants a proper drink in your hand at a party, you'll get this
42The movement

[Group] who [value] are switching to [category]

 Runners who take recovery seriously are switching their post-run drink Founders who need clear-headed Mondays are switching their Friday drink
43The future self

Future you, [time] from now, will thank you for [small action today]

 Future you, six months from now, will thank you for fixing your sleep this week Next-Sunday you will thank you for this
44Them vs you

Some people [old way]. You [new way].

 Some people need four coffees to function. You'd rather not. Some people measure a good night by the hangover. You don't.
45The quiet flex

The [small thing] that says [desirable trait] about you

 The morning ritual that says you've got your life together The drink order that says you're not missing out, you've levelled up
46The permission slip

You're allowed to [want something] without [guilt or consequence]

 You're allowed to want energy without the jitters You're allowed to love the pub without the hangover
47The life-stage call-out

If you're [life stage] and [change you've noticed], this is for you

 If you're in your 40s and your recovery isn't what it was, this is for you If you're 30-something and hangovers now last two days, this is for you
48The values match

Made for people who [value], not people who [opposite]

 Made for people who want results, not another 12-step routine Made for people who love the taste of a good drink, not the after-effects
49Their own label

[The label your persona uses for themselves], this one's for you: [situation]

 Early-morning gym crowd, this one's for you: no more 5am caffeine shakes Sober-curious crew, this one's for you: wedding season, sorted
50The identity shift

I stopped being [old identity] the day I [action]

 I stopped being “the tired one” the day I fixed my mornings I stopped being “the one who always overdoes it” the day I found a drink I actually liked
💡

Cast for the call-out. Identity hooks work best when the creator visibly belongs to the group you're naming. The face sells the hook before the words do.

Most aware Objection & urgency

Section 6: Objection & urgency hooks

10 formulas for most-aware audiences

Most-aware audiences know you, like what they've seen and are close to buying. They've visited, watched, maybe added to basket. They don't need more information, they need a final reason to act. Your job now is to handle the one objection holding them back, or to show them what waiting is costing them.

Use real consequences, not fake countdown timers. Invented scarcity on a product you restock every week kills trust faster than it converts.

HookFill-in templateHealth & wellnessAlcohol-free
51The objection, out loud

“[Exact objection]?” Fair question.

 “Isn't it just expensive vitamins?” Fair question. “Why pay that much for a drink with no alcohol?” Fair question.
52The price reframe

It's [£X] a day. Less than your [everyday purchase].

 It's £1.20 a day. Less than your oat flat white. It costs less than a round, and nobody's hungover tomorrow.
53The risk reversal

Try it for [time]. If [outcome] doesn't happen, [guarantee].

 Try it for 30 days. If your afternoons don't change, it's free. Try the mixed case. If you don't find a favourite, we'll refund you.
54The fence-sitter

Still thinking about it? Here's what [X] people decided last [time].

 Still thinking about it? 900 people started their first tub last week. Still on the fence? Here's what changed for the people who tried it last month.
55The cost of waiting

Every [time] you wait, [compounding consequence]

 Every week you put off fixing your sleep is another week of foggy mornings Every weekend you wait is another Sunday lost to the sofa
56The real deadline

[Real event] is [time] away. Start now and [outcome by then].

 Summer is eight weeks away. Start now and feel the difference by your holiday. Dry January starts in three weeks. Stock up now so you're not caught out.
57You already know

You already know [current approach] isn't working. So what's next?

 You already know another energy drink isn't the answer. So what's next? You already know “just the one” never is. So what's next?
58The FAQ

The question everyone asks before they buy: [question]

 The question everyone asks before they buy: will it actually taste OK? The question everyone asks before they buy: will anyone notice it's 0%?
59The basket nudge

You left something behind. [Reason or incentive to return].

 You left your gut reset in the basket. Here's 15% off to finish what you started. Your mixed case is still waiting. Here's free delivery to make it easy.
60The honest tally

What is [problem] actually costing you right now?

 What's bad sleep actually costing you? Your mood, your training, your patience. What are your weekends actually costing you? Add it up.
💡

Match the objection to the drop-off point. Product page viewers usually need proof. Basket abandoners usually need the price or the risk handled. Check where they left before you choose the hook.

Section 8: How to test hooks

A hook bank is a starting point. Testing turns it into an advantage.

  1. 01

    Start with the persona, not the hook

    Pick one persona and one angle first. Then pull three to five hooks of different types that all argue for that same angle.

  2. 02

    Change one thing only

    Same body, same edit, same offer. Only the first three seconds change. Otherwise you can't tell whether the hook won or something else did.

  3. 03

    Judge hooks on hook metrics

    Read the 3-second hook rate first, then the 15-second hold rate. CPA and ROAS matter, but they're shaped by the whole ad, not just the opening line.

  4. 04

    Give it enough spend to speak

    Don't kill a hook after a day. Let every variation spend evenly until the numbers settle into a pattern, then make the call.

  5. 05

    Iterate on the winner before moving on

    Give the winning hook type new visuals, a new persona and new wording before you test a different type. Squeeze every learning out of what already works.

  6. 06

    Log every result

    Losers teach as much as winners. Every test should feed the next brief, so over time you build a hook playbook nobody else has: one based on how your audience actually behaves.
